# Image Preparation

Good photos make a fundraising page. Prepare them before the site is built.

## Contents

1. Gather
2. Crop and clean
3. Make web copies
4. Name and describe
5. Logos
6. Photos of people

## 1. Gather

Collect a hero photo (the car, ideally with the team), a few more car photos, team photos, and logos. Keep an untouched original of every file. Attach the real files to Claude. A description of a photo does not supply the photo.

## 2. Crop and clean

Crop in a free editor such as GIMP. Straighten the horizon, trim distractions, and keep the car large in the frame. A wide image works best for the hero, and a squarer crop for the gallery.

## 3. Make web copies

Compress copies with Squoosh (free, in the browser). Compare the result with the original before saving. Suggested sizes:

| Use | Width | Format | Target size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | 1600 px | JPG or WebP | under 300 KB |
| Gallery and car | 1000 px | JPG or WebP | under 150 KB |
| Sponsor logos | up to 400 px wide | SVG or PNG | under 50 KB |

Keep a high-quality master of each image.

## 4. Name and describe

Use short, descriptive file names, for example `team-car-front.jpg`, not `IMG_4021.jpg`. Write alt text for each image: one plain sentence saying what is shown, for example "The team's red Land Cruiser parked at the start line, decorated with charity stickers." Decorative images get empty alt text.

## 5. Logos

Prepare vector logos in Inkscape if you can. Save as SVG, or a transparent PNG. Use a sponsor's logo only with their permission, and the charity's logo only as the charity allows.

## 6. Photos of people

Ask the people shown before publishing. Take extra care with children: get a parent or guardian's permission, and avoid full names. If in doubt, choose a photo of the car instead.
